**Title:** Examining the Convergence of Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models: A Quantitative Analysis

**Abstract:** This paper derives a precise upper bound for the Wasserstein distance between a distribution learned by a diffusion model and the target distribution. Unlike previous work, it avoids assumptions about the learned score function and applies to distributions lacking a density relative to the Lebesgue measure, with the bound not exponentially dependent on the ambient space dimension.  The results build upon and improve existing research.

**Introduction:**  The paper focuses on denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) and their relationship to score-based generative models (SGMs).  It highlights the implicit score function approximation in DDPMs and their unification under stochastic differential equations (SDEs). The paper proposes a novel approach, adapting methods from variational autoencoders (VAEs) to DDPMs, to derive quantitative Wasserstein-based upper bounds without assumptions on the score function or data distribution.  It contrasts this approach with existing research that relies on restrictive assumptions or yields non-quantitative bounds.

**Methodology:** The paper uses a discrete-time formulation of DDPMs, avoiding the SDE framework used in previous research. It defines a forward and a backward process, with assumptions made about the Lipschitz continuity of the backward process's mean functions.  The approach involves upper-bounding the Wasserstein distance W1(µ, πθ(·)) by separately bounding W1(µ, µθⁿ) and W1(µθⁿ, πθ(·)), where µ is the target distribution, πθ(·) is the learned distribution, and µθⁿ is an empirical regenerated distribution.  The bounds are derived using techniques inspired by VAE analysis and PAC-Bayesian bounds.

**Results:** The main result is Theorem 3.1, which provides a quantitative upper bound on the Wasserstein distance W1(µ, πθ(·)). This bound depends on a reconstruction loss computed over a finite sample, KL divergence between the forward process and the prior, and Lipschitz constants related to the backward process. The theorem is discussed in detail, including the implications of the various terms and the impact of the hyperparameter λ. The theorem is then specialized to the forward process of Ho et al. (2020), providing a more concrete instance of the bound.  Lemmas 3.2, 3.3, 3.4, and 3.5 are key components of the proof of Theorem 3.1.

**Conclusion:** The paper successfully derives a quantitative upper bound on the Wasserstein distance for DDPMs without relying on assumptions about the score function or restrictive assumptions on the data distribution.  The bound is not exponentially dependent on the dimension, offering a significant improvement over previous theoretical analyses of diffusion models.  The approach uses relatively simple proofs compared to existing SDE-based approaches.


**Section Relationships:** The Introduction motivates the research by outlining existing limitations in the theoretical understanding of DDPMs and highlighting the paper's contribution. The Methodology details the novel approach and assumptions used to derive the bounds. The Results present the main theorem and its implications, supported by lemmas that are crucial parts of the proof.  The Abstract concisely summarizes the key findings and contributions. The Conclusion restates the key achievements and their significance.
