**Title:** Advancements in 3D Food Modeling: A Review of the MetaFood Challenge Techniques and Outcomes

**Abstract:** This study introduces the MetaFood Challenge, addressing the lack of high-fidelity 3D food data and collaborative efforts in the field.  The challenge aimed to generate accurate 3D food models from 2D images using a checkerboard for calibration, testing 16 teams across three difficulty levels (200, 30, and 1 image).  Encouraging results demonstrate the potential for improved portion estimation in dietary evaluations.

**Introduction:** The paper highlights the need for precise and scalable food portion estimation techniques for dietary monitoring and nutritional assessment.  It contrasts current manual methods (24-Hour Recall, FFQs) with the potential of computer vision-based 3D modeling for more accurate and efficient assessments. The MetaFood Challenge is presented as a key initiative to advance this technology, focusing on creating accurate 3D models from various visual inputs.

**Methodology:** The MetaFood Challenge used a dataset of 20 food items with varying image counts (easy: ~200, medium: 30, hard: 1) and a checkerboard for scale calibration.  Evaluation occurred in two phases: Phase I assessed volume accuracy using Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE); Phase II (for top performers) assessed shape accuracy using Chamfer distance.  Two items were excluded from the final evaluation due to data quality issues.

**Results:** The paper presents results from the top three teams (VolETA, ININ-VIAUN, and FoodRiddle).  VolETA, employing multi-view reconstruction and deep learning, achieved the best overall performance. ININ-VIAUN utilized COLMAP and single-view reconstruction methods (ZeroNVS) depending on the number of images. FoodRiddle excelled with a structure-from-motion approach for multi-view and advanced methods like LGM for single-view reconstruction.  Each team's methodology and quantitative results (MAPE and Chamfer distance) are detailed.

**Conclusion:** The MetaFood Challenge successfully advanced 3D food reconstruction techniques, demonstrating the feasibility of accurate portion and shape estimations. The diverse approaches employed by the top teams showcased the potential of this technology for nutritional analysis and dietary assessment, establishing a foundation for future research.
