**Title:** Detailed Action Identification in Baseball Game Recordings

**Abstract:** This research introduces MLB-YouTube, a new dataset for nuanced baseball activity recognition, supporting segmented and continuous video analysis.  The study evaluates methods for activity recognition, focusing on temporal dynamics, and assesses pitch velocity/type forecasting. Results show that incorporating temporal dynamics improves detailed activity recognition.

**Introduction:**  The paper addresses action recognition in sports videos, specifically baseball.  Existing systems like PITCHf/x have limitations due to data accessibility.  MLB-YouTube, a new dataset with fine-grained annotations from broadcast videos, is introduced to address this, focusing on subtle activity differences.  The study compares temporal feature aggregation methods for both segmented and continuous video analysis.

**Methodology:** The MLB-YouTube dataset, composed of segmented and continuous video clips from 20 MLB games, is described.  For segmented videos, several temporal feature aggregation techniques are explored: max/mean pooling, fixed temporal pyramids, temporal convolutions, and learned sub-events (Gaussian-based pooling).  For continuous videos, these methods are adapted using a sliding window approach, and a "super-event" representation (Cauchy distributions) is introduced to model global video context.  Models are trained using I3D and InceptionV3 CNNs, with performance evaluated using mAP (mean average precision) for multi-label classification and RMSE for pitch speed regression.  Pitch type classification uses pose data from OpenPose.

**Results:**  Experiments on segmented videos show that learning sub-events significantly improves multi-label classification accuracy, surpassing other temporal pooling methods.  Pitch speed regression achieves an average error of 3.6 mph using a high frame rate (60fps). Pitch type classification achieves 34.5% accuracy using I3D and 36.4% with pose data.  For continuous videos, combining sub- and super-event representations yields the best performance, outperforming other methods.

**Conclusion:**  The study demonstrates the effectiveness of incorporating temporal dynamics, particularly learned sub-events, for detailed activity recognition in baseball videos. The MLB-YouTube dataset and the proposed methods contribute to advancements in sports video analysis.

**Section Relationships:** The Introduction motivates the research by highlighting the need for improved baseball video analysis and introduces the MLB-YouTube dataset. The Methodology describes the dataset and the methods used to analyze it. The Results present the findings of the experiments, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed methods. The Conclusion summarizes the key findings and contributions of the research. The Abstract provides a concise overview of the entire paper.
