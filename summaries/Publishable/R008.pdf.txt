**Title:** Advanced Techniques for Through and Contextually Interpreting Noun-Noun Compounds

**Abstract:** This study investigates the effectiveness of transfer learning (TL) and multi-task learning (MTL) for interpreting noun-noun compounds. Experiments show that TL (initializing parameters) and MTL (parameter sharing) improve a neural classifier's generalization across a dataset with uneven semantic relationship distribution.  Using dual annotations (two relation sets) further enhances precision and F1 scores for less common relations.

**Introduction:**  Noun-noun compound interpretation, a crucial NLP task, is challenging due to the high productivity of the linguistic structure and the difficulty in deriving semantics from constituents. This research applies TL and MTL to address these challenges, aiming to improve accuracy and generalization, particularly for infrequent semantic relations.  The highly skewed distribution of relations in the chosen dataset makes it ideal for evaluating TL and MTL.

**Methodology:** The study uses a noun-noun compound dataset with dual annotations (PCEDT and NomBank relations).  A feed-forward neural network is used as a baseline (single-task learning – STL).  TL is implemented by transferring weights from a model trained on one relation set to initialize a model for the other. MTL involves training a single model on both relation sets simultaneously, with variations in shared layers (embedding, hidden).  The Adam optimizer and negative-log likelihood loss function are used.  Model performance is evaluated using accuracy and F1 scores.

**Results:**  Results show that TL and MTL improve accuracy, especially on the test set, indicating better generalization.  However, accuracy alone is insufficient; analysis of per-relation F1 scores reveals that MTL, especially with fully shared layers (MTLF), can negatively impact performance on some relations.  TL and MTL, particularly when focusing on the embedding layer, significantly boost F1 scores for less frequent relations.  The improvements are more pronounced when transferring from NomBank to PCEDT. Analysis reveals a strong correlation between lexical memorization and model performance, especially for high-frequency relations.

**Conclusion:** TL and MTL, especially when applied to the embedding layer, effectively mitigate class imbalance and improve the prediction of low-frequency relations in noun-noun compound interpretation.  MTL with fully shared layers can be detrimental. The study highlights the importance of considering both accuracy and F1 scores, particularly in imbalanced datasets.  Future work will explore incorporating additional NLP tasks within the same frameworks.

**Section Relationships:** The Introduction motivates the research by highlighting the challenges of noun-noun compound interpretation and the potential of TL and MTL. The Methodology describes the experimental setup, including the dataset, model, and TL/MTL implementations. The Results present the quantitative findings (accuracy and F1 scores) and qualitative analysis (error analysis and lexical memorization). The Conclusion summarizes the findings and suggests future research directions.  The Abstract provides a concise overview of the entire study.
