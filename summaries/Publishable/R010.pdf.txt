**Title:** Detecting Medication Usage in Parkinson’s Disease Through Multi-modal Indoor Positioning: A Pilot Study in a Naturalistic Environment

**Abstract:** This paper proposes a transformer-based method (MDCSA) using RSSI and accelerometer data from wearable sensors to improve indoor localization accuracy and detect Parkinson's Disease (PD) medication ("on" vs. "off") states based on in-home gait speed.  A five-day study in a smart home with 24 participants (12 PD patients and 12 controls) showed MDCSA outperformed other methods in localization, and derived gait speed features accurately predicted medication state.

**Introduction:** Parkinson's disease (PD) causes motor fluctuations impacting gait, especially with levodopa treatment.  Current clinical evaluations are limited. This research aims to leverage indoor localization, using data from wearable sensors, to provide continuous monitoring of in-home mobility as a biomarker for PD progression and medication effectiveness.  The study uses RSSI and accelerometer data from wearable devices to enhance the accuracy of indoor localization, focusing on the impact of medication on gait speed between rooms in a home environment.

**Methodology:** The study utilized a novel deep neural network, Multihead Dual Convolutional Self-Attention (MDCSA),  to process RSSI and accelerometer data simultaneously.  MDCSA incorporates positional embedding, dual convolutional self-attention to capture local and global temporal dynamics, and a conditional random field (CRF) layer for room-level prediction. A binary classification layer targets specific room transitions (hallway usage) to refine predictions.  The loss function combines negative log-likelihood and binary cross-entropy. The model was compared against Random Forest, a modified transformer encoder, and a state-of-the-art multimodal time series model.

**Results:** MDCSA significantly outperformed other methods in room-level localization accuracy across various cross-validation schemes, including those with limited training data.  In-home gait speed features (room-to-room transition duration and frequency), derived from MDCSA’s localization output, accurately classified PD patients' medication states, showing substantial improvement over demographic features alone.  Statistical significance was confirmed through the Friedman and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests.

**Conclusion:** MDCSA provides accurate indoor localization and enables the extraction of meaningful in-home gait speed features that effectively differentiate "on" and "off" medication states in PD patients.  This approach offers potential for continuous monitoring of PD symptoms and medication response in real-world settings.
