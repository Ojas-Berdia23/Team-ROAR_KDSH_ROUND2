**Title:** The Importance of Written Explanations in Aggregating Crowdsourced Predictions

**Abstract:** This research demonstrates that incorporating written justifications alongside predictions significantly improves the accuracy of aggregated crowdsourced forecasts, especially before the final phase of a question's lifespan.  The study analyzes factors differentiating reliable and unreliable justifications.

**Introduction:** The study builds upon the "wisdom of the crowd" concept, acknowledging its limitations. It focuses on aggregating forecasts for political, economic, and social questions, where each forecast includes a prediction and a written justification. The research aims to improve the "calling" of questions (determining a final aggregated prediction) by leveraging both predictions and justifications.  Key terms are defined (question, forecaster, forecast, prediction, justification, calling a question), and the research questions are outlined, focusing on the value of past forecasts, written justifications, and the effect of time on prediction accuracy.

**Methodology:** The study uses data from the Good Judgment Open platform, comprising 441 binary questions and 96,664 forecasts.  Experiments compare majority vote and weighted vote baselines against a neural network model. The neural network processes questions and forecasts (predictions and justifications represented using BERT embeddings), employing an LSTM to aggregate information.  Ablation studies examine the contribution of different input components (prediction, question, justification). Accuracy is the evaluation metric, assessed across the entire question lifespan and its four quartiles.  The data is split into training, validation, and test sets, carefully avoiding data leakage.

**Results:** Results show that including justifications significantly improves accuracy, especially when combined with question information in the neural network model. The neural network outperforms baselines in the first three quartibles of a question's lifespan. Using "active" forecasts (latest from each forecaster) generally improves performance over using only "daily" forecasts.  However, in the final quartile, baselines outperform the neural network.  Analysis of question difficulty (based on baseline performance) reveals that the neural network's advantage is more pronounced for more difficult questions. Qualitative analysis of justifications reveals that shorter justifications and references to previous forecasts are associated with lower accuracy.

**Conclusion:**  Aggregating weighted predictions is a strong baseline. However, incorporating questions and justifications, especially within the developed neural network model, significantly improves accuracy, particularly in the early stages of a question's lifespan. The method avoids profiling individual forecasters, highlighting the potential for robust, privacy-preserving aggregation.

**Relationship between sections:** The Introduction sets the context and research questions. The Methodology describes the data and the models used to answer those questions. The Results section presents the findings from applying these models to the data. The Conclusion summarizes the main findings and their implications, drawing on the Results and referencing the context provided in the Introduction.
