**Title:** Addressing Min-Max Challenges in Nonconvex-Nonconcave Problems with Solutions Exhibiting Weak Minty Properties

**Abstract:** This research analyzes structured nonconvex-nonconcave min-max problems with "weak Minty solutions," a recently defined property generalizing monotonicity.  It establishes a 1/k convergence rate for a modified optimistic gradient method (OGDA+, Algorithm 1) in the squared operator norm, matching the extragradient method (EG).  A novel adaptive step-size EG+ (Algorithm 3) is also introduced, eliminating the need for problem-specific parameters.

**Introduction:** The paper addresses the limited theoretical understanding of nonconvex-nonconcave min-max problems prevalent in machine learning (GANs, adversarial learning).  It focuses on a subset of these problems exhibiting weak Minty solutions (Assumption 1), a less restrictive condition than existing assumptions like monotonicity or Minty solutions.  The research investigates whether OGDA can achieve convergence comparable to EG for problems with weak Minty solutions.

**Methodology:** The paper proposes a modified OGDA (OGDA+) with a parameter γ (Algorithm 1) and an adaptive step-size EG+ (Algorithm 3).  Convergence analysis is performed for both methods, deriving convergence rates and conditions on step sizes (Theorems 3.1, 3.2, 3.3, 4.1). A stochastic variant of OGDA+ (Algorithm 2) is also analyzed. The analysis considers the Lipschitz continuity of the operator F derived from the min-max problem and the weak Minty parameter ρ.

**Results:**  For OGDA+, a convergence rate of O(1/k) in the squared operator norm is proven (Theorem 3.1), matching EG+. Improved bounds are obtained under monotonicity (Theorem 3.2). A stochastic variant is shown to require batch sizes of O(ϵ−1) (Theorem 3.3).  The adaptive step-size EG+ achieves similar convergence guarantees without prior knowledge of the Lipschitz constant (Theorem 4.1).


**Conclusion:** The paper demonstrates that a modified OGDA and an adaptive step-size EG can achieve comparable convergence rates to EG for problems with weak Minty solutions. While OGDA+ generally offers minimal advantage over EG+, it shows superior performance in specific problem scenarios (example (7)). The research highlights that the typical minimization paradigm of smaller step sizes for convergence and larger step sizes for speed doesn't directly translate to min-max problems, especially for weak Minty solutions, where adaptive step sizes are beneficial.

**Section Interrelation:** The Introduction motivates the research problem and introduces weak Minty solutions. The Methodology describes the proposed algorithms and their theoretical analysis. The Results present the proven convergence rates and conditions. The Conclusion summarizes the findings and discusses open questions. The numerical experiments (section 5, not summarized in detail to keep it concise) support the theoretical findings.
