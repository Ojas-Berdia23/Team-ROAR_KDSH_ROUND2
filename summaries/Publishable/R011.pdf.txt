**Title:** Addressing Popularity Bias with Popularity-Conscious Alignment and Contrastive Learning

**Abstract:** This paper addresses popularity bias in Collaborative Filtering (CF) recommendation systems.  Existing methods struggle to leverage supervisory signals from popular items to improve unpopular item representations and reduce representation separation caused by bias.  The proposed method, Popularity-Aware Alignment and Contrast (PAAC), uses a supervised alignment module to leverage supervisory signals from popular items to improve unpopular item representations.  A re-weighting system in the contrastive learning module addresses representation separation by considering item popularity. Experiments on three real-world datasets demonstrate PAAC's effectiveness in reducing popularity bias.

**Introduction:** Collaborative filtering (CF) is a common technique in recommender systems, but suffers from popularity bias, leading to accuracy discrepancies between popular and unpopular items. This bias hinders accurate preference understanding and exacerbates the Matthew effect.  The paper highlights two key challenges in mitigating this bias: inadequate representation of unpopular items and representation separation between popular and unpopular items in the embedding space.

**Methodology:** PAAC addresses these challenges with two modules: (1) A supervised alignment module uses shared supervisory signals from popular items to improve the representations of unpopular items, aligning representations of items interacted with by the same user.  This is formulated as a loss function (LSA) that minimizes the distance between popular and unpopular item representations for users who interacted with both. (2) A re-weighting contrast module adjusts weights in the contrastive learning loss function (LCL) to reduce representation separation based on item popularity, using hyperparameters α and β to control the weighting of popular and unpopular items as positive and negative samples respectively.  The items are dynamically grouped into popular and unpopular sets within each mini-batch using a hyperparameter x. The overall loss function combines recommendation loss (LREC), LSA, and LCL.

**Results:** Experiments on Amazon-book, Yelp2018, and Gowalla datasets compare PAAC against several baseline debiasing methods using Recall@K, HR@K, and NDCG@K metrics. PAAC consistently outperforms baselines, showing significant improvements, particularly on Yelp2018.  Ablation studies confirm the contribution of each component (alignment and re-weighting) in PAAC.  Analysis across different popularity groups shows PAAC significantly improves the recommendation of unpopular items and reduces the performance gap between popular and unpopular items.  Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis explores the impact of λ1, λ2, α, β, and x, showing that optimal performance is achieved with appropriate weighting of the loss functions and a balanced grouping ratio.

**Conclusion:** PAAC effectively reduces popularity bias by leveraging supervisory signals from popular items to improve unpopular item representations and mitigating representation separation through re-weighted contrastive learning. Future work will explore more task-specific alignment and contrast adjustments and extend the approach to other biases.
